Flat 2D vector cartoon, the look of a simple Vietnamese history cartoon series for YouTube,
drawn in Flash or Toon Boom. Television animation cels. NOT painted background art, NOT concept
art, NOT illustration.

LINE. THIN, clean, even vector outlines in dark warm brown. Smooth confident curves, no sketch
lines, no varying thickness, no bold heavy ink. The outline is a thin contour, not a border.
Outlines appear only on real shape boundaries, never as interior detail scribble.

BRIGHTNESS. Mid key, the brightness of a clear ordinary day. Not dim and not washed out. Keep
real mid tones in the greens and the earth; do not pastel everything. Dark areas are the outlines
plus a few genuinely dark objects, around one tenth of the frame. No dark corners, no vignette,
no deep shadow masses, no murky undergrowth, and equally no large empty expanse of pale sky.

COLOUR. Soft natural tones at MODERATE saturation, closer to pastel than to poster paint. Muted
sage and grass greens, pale ochre, soft brick red, warm light grey, light sky blue, pale blue
distance. Never deep jewel colours, never heavy browns, never neon.

FLATNESS. Very few distinct colours in the whole picture. Each object is one flat fill plus at
most one slightly darker tone of the same hue. No gradients except a gentle one in the open sky.
No cast shadows on the ground. No highlights, no rim light, no ambient occlusion, no texture, no
grain, no hatching, no speckling, no painterly brushwork, no pebbles or clods drawn on the earth.
Ground is a smooth flat band of colour.

DETAIL, the single most important rule. The scene is FULL and specific: many objects and many
people, the way a whole street or a whole village is full. Do NOT empty the scene out. What is
simple is each individual object, not the scene. Draw every thing with the fewest possible
shapes: a house is a roof shape, a wall shape and two window rectangles; a soldier is a head, a
body and four limbs; the earth is one flat band. Complexity comes from the NUMBER of simple
things, never from detail inside one thing. No texture, no rendering, no pebbles or clods on the
ground, no individual leaves, no decorative debris.

FIGURES. Head is noticeably large for the body, about one to five head-to-body. Face is
completely flat: small solid black oval eyes with no white and no iris detail, two thin short
eyebrow arcs, a very small nose line or none at all, one short mouth line. One flat skin tone and
nothing else on the face, no cheek shading, no jawline modelling, no nose bridge. Hair is one
flat black shape. Hands are simple mitten shapes. Not chibi, not caricature, not stick figures,
not handsome manga faces.
ETHNICITY follows each character's side, never one face for everyone: Vietnamese characters have
Vietnamese features and black hair; Southern Han soldiers and officers are drawn as Han Chinese.

PERIOD: Northern Vietnam (Tĩnh Hải quân), year 938 AD, end of the Tang era. NOT the Đông Sơn bronze age.
VIETNAMESE SIDE: fighters in plain dark brown or indigo knee-length tunics with a cloth belt, loose trousers
rolled to the knee or bare legs, a dark cloth headband or turban, hair in a bun, barefoot or straw sandals;
iron-tipped spears, long-handled sabres, bows, round rattan shields; small fast wooden boats paddled by
six to ten men. Leaders wear a simple iron lamellar vest over the tunic and a dark turban.
RECURRING CHARACTERS, draw them the same every time:
- NGÔ QUYỀN: a Vietnamese general about forty, strong build, short black beard and moustache, dark
  turban, iron lamellar vest over a deep red tunic, a sabre at his hip.
- DƯƠNG ĐÌNH NGHỆ: an older Vietnamese governor, grey beard, dark green official robe and black cap.
- KIỀU CÔNG TIỄN: a thin Vietnamese officer with a narrow moustache and shifty eyes, black robe.
- LƯU HOẰNG THÁO: a young Southern Han prince, clean-shaven, ornate gilded lamellar armour, red cape,
  helmet with a red plume.
SOUTHERN HAN SIDE (Chinese, Tang style): soldiers in lamellar armour over red tunics with round iron
helmets, halberds and crossbows; large wooden war junks with square batten sails, several decks, painted
eyes on the bow, plain red banners with NO writing.
ABSOLUTELY NOT: Đông Sơn bronze drums or bronze armour, Japanese samurai, Ming/Qing costume, Korean hanbok,
conical straw hats on generals. NOTHING JAPANESE: no katana, no round sword guards, no ninja look,
no Japanese stone lanterns, no torii, no tatami. Swords are straight Tang-style blades (jian or dao) with a
small bar cross-guard. No blue-and-white porcelain (it did not exist yet); plain brown or green glazed pottery only.

NATURE: the Red River delta and its edge: green rice fields, a wide red-brown river, low green hills
and a few limestone peaks on the horizon, bamboo clumps, banana trees, stilt houses with curved
boat-shaped thatched roofs (the roof ridge sweeps up at both ends). A tree
canopy is ONE scalloped bumpy silhouette in mid green plus two or three lighter blobs; never draw
individual leaves. The Bạch Đằng estuary: a very wide brown tidal river mouth, low muddy banks with mangroves and reeds, limestone karst islets toward the sea.

COMPOSITION. Wide establishing shot, 16:9, camera slightly ELEVATED looking down at a three
quarter angle so the ground reads as a broad horizontal plane. The horizon sits HIGH: sky fills
at most a third of the frame, the rest is ground, people and objects. Figures spread across the
width like actors on a stage, a few larger ones near the lower edge, many smaller simplified ones
behind them.

LIGHT. Flat and even, no light source, no time-of-day drama. For night, shift the whole palette
to a light blue-grey and keep everything clearly readable; never crush to black.

Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, captions, subtitles, logos, watermarks, speech bubbles,
signatures or UI anywhere in the image. No modern objects. No photorealism, no 3D render, no
anime or manga background art, no Ghibli painted background, no American comic style, no Chinese
or Japanese costume on any Vietnamese character.
